Crypto trading bot development, built around your strategy

We code the crypto strategy you define into a reliable bot: grid, DCA, signal, copy-trading and market-making bots for Binance, Bybit, Coinbase, Kraken and OKX, backtested first and run with hard risk limits.

Before we start: what a crypto bot can and cannot do

A crypto trading bot follows rules faster and more consistently than a person can, around the clock. It does not find profit on its own. If the strategy loses money by hand, it will lose money automatically. We build and test the strategy you bring us, we tell you plainly when a backtest looks too good to be true, and we never promise returns. Crypto trading carries a real risk of loss.

Crypto bots we build

  • Grid bots that place buy and sell orders at set price levels, with rules for when the price leaves the range.
  • DCA bots that buy in steps as the price moves and take profit at a target, with a cap on total exposure.
  • Signal and webhook bots that turn TradingView alerts or your own indicators into exchange orders.
  • Copy-trading bots that mirror trades from a lead account to your other accounts, with size scaling and per-account limits.
  • Market-making bots that quote both sides of the order book with inventory and spread limits.
  • Cross-exchange arbitrage bots that watch price gaps between exchanges and only act when the gap is larger than fees and slippage (see arbitrage bot development).
  • Solana and DEX bots that swap through aggregators such as Jupiter for strategies you define, controlled from Telegram.
  • Freqtrade and Hummingbot customisation: if you already run an open-source bot, we write strategies, connectors and dashboards for it.

How a crypto trading bot works

  1. Market dataCandles and order books over REST and WebSocket streams
  2. StrategyYour entry, exit and sizing rules
  3. Risk enginePosition caps, daily loss limit, max open trades, kill switch
  4. ExecutionOrders with retries, partial fills, precision and rate limits handled
  5. MonitorDatabase of trades, Telegram alerts, dashboard, auto-restart

The risk engine sits between your strategy and the exchange: every order is checked against your limits before it is sent.

Exchanges and APIs

We connect to Binance, Bybit, Coinbase Advanced, Kraken, OKX, KuCoin and others through their official APIs, usually via the ccxt library plus exchange-specific code where needed (for futures, funding rates or special order types). Each exchange has its own symbols, fees, minimum order sizes and rate limits, so every one is tested separately, first on its testnet or with paper trading where available.

Features we build into every crypto bot

  • Backtesting on historical data with fees and slippage included, and checks against look-ahead mistakes
  • Paper trading mode that runs the live code without real orders
  • Risk limits: maximum position size, daily loss limit, maximum open trades, kill switch
  • State recovery: the bot remembers open orders and positions after a restart
  • Telegram alerts and commands such as /status, /pause and /close
  • Trade history in a database, and a web dashboard when you need one
  • API keys stored securely, trade-only, with withdrawal permission off and IP allow-listing where the exchange supports it

Tech stack

Python (ccxt, pandas, asyncio, FastAPI) for most bots; Node.js where WebSocket throughput or an existing codebase calls for it; Freqtrade and Hummingbot when an open-source base fits; Solana web3 libraries for DEX work; PostgreSQL or TimescaleDB for trade and candle history; Docker on a small cloud server (AWS, DigitalOcean or Hetzner) located near the exchange's servers.

How we work

  1. Strategy spec. You describe the rules; we write them back as a precise spec with every "it depends" answered.
  2. Backtest. We code the strategy and run it on historical data with fees, so you can judge it before going further.
  3. Paper trade. The live bot runs without real orders until its results match the backtest closely enough for you.
  4. Small live start. Real orders with small sizes and tight limits, on your exchange account and your server.
  5. Handover and support. Code, documentation and a runbook; we can keep it updated when exchanges change their APIs.

Risks and compliance

  • Crypto prices can move sharply, exchanges can go down and APIs can change without notice. Limits and a kill switch reduce the damage; they cannot remove the risk.
  • You are responsible for following your exchange's terms of service and the laws where you live, including tax. Some exchanges are not available in some countries.
  • If other people will connect their accounts or follow your trades for a fee, you may be offering a regulated service. Get legal advice before launch; we build the software, not the licence.
  • We do not build front-running, sandwich or meme-coin sniper bots, wash-trading or volume-faking bots, or anything meant to manipulate a market.

Questions clients ask

How much does it cost to build a crypto trading bot?

At $50 per hour, a signal or webhook bot on one exchange is usually 30 to 60 hours ($1,500 to $3,000), a grid or DCA bot with a settings panel and Telegram alerts 60 to 120 hours ($3,000 to $6,000), and arbitrage or market-making systems 150 to 300 hours or more ($7,500 to $15,000+).

Will a crypto trading bot make money?

No one can promise that. A bot only automates the strategy you define. We backtest and paper trade so you can judge the strategy first, but crypto trading carries a real risk of loss.

Should I use Freqtrade or Hummingbot instead of a custom bot?

If your strategy fits their model, an open-source base is often cheaper: we write the strategy and any missing pieces. A fully custom bot makes sense when you need unusual order logic, several exchanges at once, your own dashboard or a product for other users.

Are my API keys and strategy safe?

Keys stay on your own server or cloud account, set to trade-only with withdrawals disabled and IP restrictions where possible. The code lives in your repository and we sign an NDA on request.

Which exchanges do you support?

Binance, Bybit, Coinbase Advanced, Kraken, OKX, KuCoin and most exchanges with a public trading API, plus Solana DEX aggregators such as Jupiter. Each exchange is tested separately.

How long does it take?

A simple signal bot is often two to three weeks including paper trading. A grid or DCA bot with a panel is usually four to six weeks; arbitrage and market-making systems take longer because they need more live testing.
