AI chatbots that answer from your content and know when to hand over

We build chatbots for websites, WhatsApp and support tools that answer customer questions from your own documents, book or look up records, and pass hard cases to your team.

Quick answer: we build custom AI chatbots at $50 per hour. A focused answer bot that replies from your FAQs or web pages and hands over to a person is usually 80 to 160 hours ($4,000 to $8,000, 2 to 5 weeks). A bot trained on your documents with sources and an admin panel is usually 200 to 400 hours ($10,000 to $20,000, 5 to 10 weeks). Running cost is separate: typically a few cents per conversation plus a small hosting bill. You own the code, the data and the accounts.

Why teams hire us for this

A chatbot that invents answers costs you customers. We ground every answer in your own help articles, policies and product data, show sources where it helps, and make the bot say so when it does not know.

We also build the parts that make a bot useful day to day: a dashboard of conversations, handover to a human agent, and a way for your team to update what the bot knows without calling a developer.

How we work

Every engagement starts with a short discovery phase: we learn your users, your data and your constraints, then send a written plan with milestones and a fixed estimate per milestone. You see working software every one to two weeks, and the code lives in your repository from day one.

  1. Discovery (2 to 5 days): we collect 50 to 100 real questions your customers ask, review the content the bot will read and agree what it must never do.
  2. First working bot (1 to 2 weeks): content prepared and indexed, a first version answering on a test page, and the question test set scored so we know the starting accuracy.
  3. Improve and connect: tune retrieval and prompts until the test set passes, then add handover, channels such as WhatsApp, and the systems it must read from or write to.
  4. Launch and watch: release to a share of visitors first, review the conversations it got wrong, fix them, then open it to everyone.

Our rate is $50 per hour. For fixed-scope work we quote per milestone so you know the cost before each stage starts. ZAWN has worked this way since 2014, with a 4.9 average from 356 reviews on Freelancer.com and 95% of projects delivered on time.

Questions clients ask

How long does a chatbot take?

A focused answer bot trained on your FAQs or web pages usually takes 2 to 5 weeks, including testing with your real customer questions. A bot that searches a larger set of documents and cites sources usually takes 5 to 10 weeks, and one that takes actions in your systems 3 to 6 months.

What does it cost to run?

It depends on traffic and the model. For a document-trained bot, plan for roughly 1 to 4 cents per conversation in model usage plus $20 to $100 a month for hosting and the database; channel fees such as WhatsApp are extra. We estimate the monthly bill before building and keep it down with caching, shorter prompts and smaller models for simple questions.

How do you stop the chatbot making things up?

The bot answers only from passages retrieved from your own content (retrieval-augmented generation), is told to say when it does not know, and hands the conversation to a person instead of guessing. Before every release we run it against a test set of real questions with known answers, so accuracy is measured, not assumed. Read how RAG works for the details.

Which AI model do you use?

We work with OpenAI, Anthropic Claude, Google and open-source models, and choose based on accuracy on your test questions, privacy needs and cost. Because the bot is built on your accounts, you can switch to a newer or cheaper model later without rebuilding it.

Can my team update what the bot knows?

Yes. We build an admin page where your team adds or edits documents and FAQs, and the bot uses the new content within minutes. You do not need a developer to change an answer.

Should I use a chatbot builder platform or a custom chatbot?

A no-code builder is fine for a simple FAQ bot on a small website. A custom chatbot makes sense when it must read private documents reliably, take actions in your own systems, meet data privacy requirements, or when per-conversation platform fees grow larger than the cost of owning the software. We will tell you honestly which route fits.

Who owns the chatbot and the conversation data?

You do. The code is in your repository, the bot runs in your cloud and model provider accounts, and conversation logs are stored in your database. Nothing is locked to ZAWN.
