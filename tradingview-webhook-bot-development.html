TradingView webhook bot development

We turn your TradingView alerts into real orders: a secure receiver checks each Pine Script signal, applies your risk limits and places the trade on your crypto exchange, Alpaca, Interactive Brokers or MetaTrader 5, then logs it and tells you on Telegram.

What a TradingView webhook bot does

TradingView can send an alert to a web address (a webhook) when your indicator or strategy fires. It cannot place orders at most brokers by itself. A webhook bot is the missing piece: a small program on a server that receives the alert, checks that it is genuine and sensible, and sends the order to your exchange or broker through its official API. The bot follows the rules in your alerts and the limits you set. It does not decide what to trade, and it cannot make a strategy profitable. Trading carries a real risk of loss.

What we build

  • Webhook receivers for crypto exchanges such as Binance, Bybit, Coinbase, Kraken and OKX, using their official APIs.
  • Stock and options routing to brokers with official APIs, such as Alpaca, Interactive Brokers and Schwab. More on brokers is on trading bot development.
  • TradingView to MT4/MT5 bridges: a receiver plus an Expert Advisor that collects each signal and trades it with lot, loss and hours limits. See MT5 Expert Advisor development.
  • Pine Script work: writing or fixing indicators and strategies, and building clean alert messages in JSON so the bot gets every value it needs.
  • Telegram signal bots that post each alert to you or a private channel, with or without placing orders. See Telegram and Discord bots.
  • Dashboards showing received alerts, orders, fills, open positions and errors, with a pause switch.

How a TradingView webhook bot works

  1. TradingView alertYour Pine Script fires and posts a JSON message
  2. ReceiverChecks the token and source IP, replies fast, queues the signal
  3. Risk checkDuplicates, position size, daily loss cap, trading hours
  4. Order routerExchange, broker API or MT5 Expert Advisor
  5. Log and alertsEvery signal, order and fill, with Telegram messages

The receiver answers TradingView within a second and does the trading work afterwards, because TradingView cancels a webhook request that takes longer than three seconds.

Common problems we fix

  • Repainting. Some indicators change past signals once a bar closes, so the backtest looks better than live trading. We check the script, fire alerts on bar close where it matters, and compare live alerts with the chart.
  • Duplicate or out-of-order alerts. Every signal gets an ID; repeats are ignored and an exit never runs before its entry.
  • Position drift. If a fill is partial or an order is rejected, the bot reads the actual position from the broker instead of trusting its own memory.
  • Tester versus live. Fees, spread, slippage and minimum order sizes are added so you can see how far live results may differ from the Strategy Tester.

Features we include as standard

  • A per-bot secret token in the alert, never your exchange or broker keys
  • API keys on the server with trading-only permissions and withdrawals turned off
  • Maximum position size, maximum orders per day and a daily loss cap that stop the bot
  • Paper or demo trading first, then a small live size
  • A kill switch in Telegram or the dashboard that cancels open orders and pauses trading
  • Logs of every alert received and every order sent, kept for review

Tech stack

Pine Script v6 for alerts and strategies; Python (FastAPI) or Node.js for the receiver; ccxt and exchange SDKs for crypto; Alpaca, Interactive Brokers (TWS API or IB Gateway) and Schwab Trader APIs for stocks; MQL5 for MT5 Expert Advisors; Redis or PostgreSQL for the signal queue and logs; Telegram Bot API for alerts; Docker on a small cloud server or VPS close to the exchange or broker.

Webhook bot or ready-made connector?

Hosted services that forward TradingView alerts to brokers are fine for simple setups and cost a monthly fee. A custom bot makes sense when you need your own risk rules, a broker or exchange the service does not support, MT5 with prop-firm limits, several strategies on one account, or full control of your keys and logs. For crypto, open-source frameworks are another route; we compare them in Freqtrade vs Hummingbot vs a custom bot.

Risks and compliance

  • Automated trading can lose money fast, especially with leverage. Limits reduce damage but cannot remove risk, and we never promise returns.
  • You are responsible for TradingView's terms, your exchange's or broker's terms, any prop-firm rules and the laws where you live. Selling signals or managing other people's money may need a licence.
  • We do not build bots that copy trades across prop-firm accounts against the firm's rules, exploit latency, or get around platform limits.

Questions clients ask

How much does a TradingView webhook bot cost?

At $50 per hour, a webhook bot that turns your alerts into orders on one crypto exchange or broker is usually 30 to 60 hours ($1,500 to $3,000). A TradingView to MT5 bridge with a receiver and an Expert Advisor is about 40 to 80 hours ($2,000 to $4,000). Writing or fixing a Pine Script strategy on its own is often 10 to 30 hours ($500 to $1,500).

Do I need a paid TradingView plan for webhooks?

Yes in practice. Webhook alerts are not available on the free Basic plan, and TradingView only sends webhooks from accounts with two-factor authentication turned on. Alert limits and how long alerts stay active depend on your plan, so check TradingView's current plans before we start.

Can TradingView alerts place trades in MetaTrader 5?

Not directly. TradingView posts the alert to a web address, and MT5 has no web address of its own. We build a small receiver on a server that checks and queues each signal, and an Expert Advisor in MT5 that collects the signal and places the order with your risk limits.

What happens if a webhook is missed or sent twice?

TradingView says webhooks can occasionally fail to arrive, and alerts can repeat. The receiver gives every signal an ID, ignores duplicates, checks the position the broker actually holds before each order, and alerts you on Telegram when a signal fails or the bot and the broker disagree.

Is it safe to put my exchange API key in the alert message?

No. TradingView itself warns against putting credentials in the alert body. Your exchange or broker keys stay on the server, with trading-only permissions and no withdrawals. The alert carries only a random token that lets the receiver accept it, plus an allowlist of TradingView's published sending IP addresses.

Will the bot make my strategy profitable?

No bot can promise that. The bot places the trades your alerts describe, with limits you set. Whether that makes money depends on your strategy and the market, and live results often differ from the TradingView Strategy Tester because of fees, slippage and repainting indicators.

Can you make my Pine Script indicator stop repainting?

Usually, yes. We find where the script uses values that change before a bar closes, such as unconfirmed bars or higher-timeframe data requested with lookahead, and change the signal to fire on confirmed bars only. Signals may then arrive one bar later than the repainting version showed, which is the honest picture of what you could have traded.

Can you convert my Pine Script strategy to MT5, NinjaTrader or Python?

Yes. Pine Script runs only on TradingView, so to trade without TradingView in the loop we rewrite the same rules as an MT5 Expert Advisor in MQL5, a NinjaScript strategy or a Python bot, then compare its signals bar by bar with the original script on the same data before going live.
