How much does a WhatsApp chatbot cost in 2026?

By Awais Chaudhry, 25 November 2026

A WhatsApp chatbot has two kinds of cost: what you pay once to have it built, and what you pay every month to run it. Most price lists online only show one of them, usually a monthly software subscription, and leave out Meta's own messaging fees and the AI model behind the replies. This guide puts all of it on one page, using the hour ranges we quote at $50 per hour and Meta's pricing rules as they stand in late 2026.

The short answer

  • Simple FAQ and lead bot: about 8 to 20 hours to build, so $400 to $1,000, usually ready in about a week.
  • AI assistant with one integration (bookings, payments or order lookups): about 30 to 80 hours, so $1,500 to $4,000, usually 2 to 4 weeks.
  • Support or sales system with several integrations, a CRM or helpdesk, an agent inbox and reports: about 100 to 250 hours, so $5,000 to $12,500, usually 1 to 3 months.
  • Running costs come on top: Meta's per-message fees, the AI model usage and hosting. For a small business these are often modest, but they grow with volume, so estimate them before you build.

What you are paying for when you build a WhatsApp chatbot

The build cost is mostly hours of engineering. These are the pieces that use them.

WhatsApp Business Platform setup

A business bot should run on the official WhatsApp Business Platform (the Cloud API), not on tools that automate the normal WhatsApp app. Setup means a verified Meta business, a phone number registered on the platform, a display name, and the message templates you need approved. This is a few hours of work, but it can take days of calendar time if business verification asks for extra documents.

Conversation design

Someone has to decide what the bot says first, which questions it handles, when it asks for details, and the exact moment it hands over to a person. Good conversation design is the difference between a bot customers use and one they type "agent" into straight away. Plan a day or two for this on a small bot.

The AI and its knowledge

For an AI bot, the work is connecting a language model to your real information: FAQs, price lists, policies, catalogues and documents. We use retrieval (RAG) so the bot answers from your content and says it does not know instead of inventing an answer. If you want the details, read how RAG works. More documents, more languages and stricter accuracy rules all add testing time.

Integrations

This is the biggest cost driver. Each system the bot must talk to, such as a booking calendar, a store, a payment gateway, a CRM or your own database, needs its own connection, error handling and tests. A bot that only answers questions is cheap. A bot that books a slot, takes a payment, confirms it by webhook and updates your CRM is several separate pieces of work. Our API integration team handles the common systems, and custom databases too.

Human handover and the team inbox

Your staff need somewhere to see and answer conversations the bot passes on, ideally with a short summary so the customer does not have to repeat themselves. Some businesses use an existing helpdesk; others need a simple inbox built for them. A multi-agent inbox with assignment and reports is a meaningful part of the larger budgets above.

Testing and launch

Before launch we run the bot against real customer questions in every language you serve, then read the first week of live conversations and tune the answers and handover rules. Skipping this step is the most common reason a cheap bot disappoints.

Running costs: what you pay every month

Meta's WhatsApp messaging fees

Meta charges businesses per delivered message, not per conversation, and only for messages the business sends. Messages customers send to you are not charged. The price depends on two things: the category of the message and the country of the person receiving it.

  • Marketing templates (offers, re-engagement, abandoned cart reminders) are the most expensive category in most countries.
  • Utility templates (order updates, booking confirmations, reminders) and authentication templates (one-time codes) cost less, and Meta offers volume tiers on them that lower the rate as monthly volume grows.
  • Service messages are the free-form replies your bot sends inside the 24-hour window after a customer writes to you. This changed recently: since 1 October 2026 Meta charges for service messages too, per message, after the first 1,000 service messages per business phone number each month. Utility templates sent inside that open window, which were free for a while, are also charged again.

There is still a useful free route. When a customer starts a chat from a Click-to-WhatsApp ad, Meta opens a free entry point window in which your replies and templates to that customer are not charged. Government bodies and non-profits that qualify also get service messages free for a set period. Meta can only change its rates on fixed dates each quarter and publishes a rate card by country, so check the current card for the countries your customers are in rather than relying on a figure from a blog post, including this one.

What this means in practice: a bot that mainly answers questions customers send in will stay inside the free service tier for many small businesses. A bot that sends lots of marketing broadcasts will spend far more on Meta fees than on AI. Design your flows with that in mind, for example by sending reminders as utility templates rather than marketing ones where the content genuinely is a reminder.

AI model usage

Each AI reply uses a language model, billed by the amount of text processed. For a typical customer conversation this is often a few cents, but long documents in every prompt, long chat histories and the most expensive models push it up. We keep it down by sending only the relevant parts of your documents, trimming history, and using a smaller model for simple steps. If you choose Meta's own AI agent product instead of your own model, Meta bills those replies separately by usage.

Hosting and software

A custom bot needs a small server or serverless functions, a database and somewhere to store documents. For most small and medium bots this is a modest monthly bill paid directly to the cloud provider. If you use a ready-made chatbot platform instead, you pay its monthly subscription, often per user or per contact, on top of Meta's fees.

Maintenance

Prices change, policies change, new questions appear, and Meta and AI providers update their APIs. Budget a few hours a month to review conversations and keep answers current, or set up an admin page so your team can edit the knowledge themselves. Our maintenance and support plans cover the technical side.

Custom build vs a chatbot platform subscription

Ready-made platforms let you set up a basic flow bot quickly for a monthly fee, and for a simple menu bot that can be the right choice. A custom build costs more up front but tends to win when:

  • the bot must connect to your own systems, such as a booking database, an ERP or a custom store;
  • you want AI answers grounded in your documents with your own rules for when to hand over;
  • you have many staff or contacts, so per-seat or per-contact pricing adds up;
  • you want to own the code, the data and the WhatsApp account outright.

A sensible rule: compare total cost over two years, not the first month. Our general guide to what an AI chatbot costs in 2026 walks through that comparison for website and app chatbots as well.

Five ways to keep the cost down

  1. Start with your top twenty questions. They usually cover most of the messages. Add the long tail after launch, based on real conversations.
  2. Add one integration at a time. Launch with answers and handover, then add bookings or payments as a second milestone.
  3. Use the official API from day one. Moving a number off an unofficial tool after it is blocked costs more than doing it properly.
  4. Plan your template categories. Classify reminders and confirmations correctly as utility messages and keep marketing sends to opted-in customers who want them.
  5. Give your team an admin page. Letting staff update prices and answers themselves saves paid developer hours every month.

Questions businesses ask

How much does a WhatsApp chatbot cost to build?

At $50 per hour, a focused FAQ and lead bot is usually 8 to 20 hours ($400 to $1,000). An AI assistant that also books appointments, takes payments or checks orders is usually 30 to 80 hours ($1,500 to $4,000). A support or sales system with several integrations and a team inbox is usually 100 to 250 hours ($5,000 to $12,500).

Are WhatsApp chatbot replies free?

Not entirely any more. Since 1 October 2026 Meta charges per message for service replies sent inside the 24-hour customer service window, after the first 1,000 service messages per business phone number each month. Messages customers send to you are free, and replies to customers who arrive from a Click-to-WhatsApp ad are free during the free entry point window.

What are the monthly running costs of a WhatsApp AI chatbot?

Three parts: Meta's per-message fees, which depend on message category and the customer's country; AI model usage, often a few cents per conversation; and hosting, usually a modest cloud bill. A bot that mostly answers incoming questions costs far less to run than one that sends frequent marketing broadcasts.
