Freqtrade vs Hummingbot vs a custom trading bot

An engineer's comparison of the two best-known open-source crypto bot frameworks and a bot written from scratch: what each is built for, where each gets in the way, what it costs to set up at $50/hour, and how to choose.

The short answer

Pick the tool that matches the shape of your strategy, not the one with the most GitHub stars.

  • Freqtrade suits candle-based strategies: indicators on 5-minute, 1-hour or daily candles that decide when to enter and exit. It has the strongest backtesting and optimisation tools of the three.
  • Hummingbot suits order-book strategies: market making, cross-exchange and spot-perpetual arbitrage, and liquidity provision, including some decentralised exchanges.
  • A custom bot suits everything that fits neither: unusual order logic, several accounts or exchanges managed together, your own dashboard, or a product other people will log in to.

Whichever you choose, the bot only automates the strategy you define. None of these tools finds profit on its own, and crypto trading carries a real risk of loss.

How the choice usually goes

  1. Rules on candles?Indicators decide entries and exits on a timeframe: start with Freqtrade
  2. Quoting the book?Market making, spreads or arbitrage across venues: start with Hummingbot
  3. Neither fits?Odd order types, many accounts, broker APIs or your own UI: custom bot
  4. Selling it?Other users, subscriptions or copy trading: custom platform plus legal advice

Most clients who bring a simple indicator strategy are best served by Freqtrade. We recommend a custom build only when a framework would need more workarounds than it saves.

When Freqtrade is the right base

  • Your strategy is a set of indicator rules (RSI, moving averages, Bollinger Bands, your own signals) on a fixed timeframe.
  • You want to backtest over years of candles, optimise parameters and check for look-ahead bias before risking money.
  • You want dry-run (paper trading) mode, Telegram control and a ready web interface without paying to build them.
  • You trade on exchanges Freqtrade already supports.

Typical work for us: writing the strategy class from your rules, custom stop-loss and position-sizing logic, protections (cooldowns, maximum drawdown locks), a FreqAI model if you want one, and deployment on your own server with monitoring.

When Hummingbot is the right base

  • Your strategy places and refreshes orders on both sides of the order book, with inventory and spread limits.
  • You want cross-exchange or spot-perpetual arbitrage logic that the framework already models.
  • You need a decentralised exchange that Hummingbot's Gateway already connects to.

Typical work for us: V2 controllers and scripts from your rules, a connector for an exchange that is not yet supported, risk limits, and a monitoring dashboard. For arbitrage, run the numbers first with the spread check on our arbitrage bot page: most visible gaps disappear after fees and slippage.

When a custom bot is worth the extra cost

  • The order logic does not map onto either framework (layered take-profits across accounts, unusual hedging, custom order types).
  • You trade stocks, forex or futures through a broker API, or MetaTrader (see MT5 Expert Advisors).
  • Several strategies, exchanges and accounts must share one risk engine and one dashboard.
  • Other people will use it: accounts, subscriptions or copy trading. Freqtrade's GPL-3.0 licence also has obligations if you distribute modified code, so ask a lawyer before you sell a product built on it.
  • You want full ownership of the code with no dependence on a framework's release cycle.

Risks that apply to all three

  • A good backtest is not a forecast. Fees, slippage, outages and changing markets all show up live; paper trade before going live.
  • Use exchange API keys with trading only, withdrawals disabled and IP restrictions where the exchange allows it.
  • Open-source code is only as safe as the version you run: pin versions, read release notes and do not install strategies or plugins from unknown sources.
  • You are responsible for following your exchange's terms and the laws where you live, including tax.

Questions clients ask

Is Freqtrade or Hummingbot better?

Neither is better overall. Freqtrade is stronger for indicator strategies on candles and for backtesting; Hummingbot is stronger for market making and arbitrage on order books. Choose the one whose model matches your strategy.

Is a free open-source bot good enough, or do I need a custom one?

For many single-exchange indicator strategies, Freqtrade is good enough and much cheaper than a custom build. A custom bot makes sense for unusual order logic, many accounts, broker APIs, your own dashboard or a product for other users.

Can you write my strategy for Freqtrade or Hummingbot?

Yes. Send the rules in plain words. We write them back as a precise spec, code the strategy, backtest it with fees, and run it in dry-run or paper mode before you decide to go live. A strategy for an existing setup is usually 20 to 60 hours ($1,000 to $3,000).

Can I move from Freqtrade to a custom bot later?

Yes. The strategy rules and backtest results carry over; the execution and risk code is rewritten. Many clients start on Freqtrade to validate an idea cheaply and move to custom code only if they outgrow it.

Will any of these bots make money?

No one can promise that. Each one only automates the strategy you define. We backtest and paper trade so you can judge the strategy first, but crypto trading carries a real risk of loss.
