Trading bots that execute your strategy exactly as written

We turn your trading rules into software: crypto exchange bots, MetaTrader Expert Advisors and TradingView strategies, backtested on historical data and run with strict risk controls.

Why teams hire us for this

Most trading bot failures are engineering failures, not strategy failures: orders that never filled, rate limits hit at the worst moment, a restart that forgot open positions, or a backtest that quietly used future data. We build the boring, reliable parts properly so your results reflect your strategy.

We build and test the strategy you bring us. We do not sell signals or promise returns, and we will tell you plainly if a backtest result looks too good to be true.

Stock trading bots and broker APIs (Robinhood, Alpaca, Interactive Brokers, Schwab)

A stock or options bot is only as reliable as the broker connection under it, so we build on official broker APIs and not on unofficial libraries that log in as you, which can break without warning and may break your broker's terms.

Trading bot dashboard

Most bots need a place to see what they are doing without reading logs. A typical dashboard we build shows open positions, orders and fills, profit and loss by day and by strategy, current risk limits and how close the bot is to them, error and restart history, and buttons to pause a strategy or close everything (kill switch). It is a small web app (usually React or Next.js on a FastAPI or Node.js backend) behind a login, often paired with Telegram alerts. A dashboard adds roughly 30 to 80 hours ($1,500 to $4,000) depending on charts and user roles; see dashboard development.

Using Claude, GPT or other LLMs in a trading bot

Large language models are useful around a trading bot, but we keep them away from unchecked order placement. Sensible uses: summarising news or filings into a signal your rules then check, turning a strategy written in plain English into a precise spec and code, reviewing logs and explaining what the bot did, or a chat interface that answers "what is my exposure right now?". If a model's output can trigger a trade, the order still passes through the same hard risk engine (position caps, daily loss limit, kill switch), and we log every model input and output so you can audit it. An LLM does not make a strategy profitable; it adds a step that must itself be tested. For agents more generally, see AI agent development.

Open-source frameworks or custom code?

For crypto, an open-source base such as Freqtrade or Hummingbot is often the cheaper start. Our Freqtrade vs Hummingbot vs custom bot comparison explains which strategies fit each and what setup costs.

Questions clients ask

Will the bot make money?

No developer can promise that, and you should be wary of anyone who does. We build your strategy exactly as specified, test it on historical data, and run it on paper trading first so you can judge it before risking money.

Is my strategy kept private?

Yes. We sign an NDA on request, the code lives in your repository, and API keys stay in your own server or cloud account with withdrawal permission switched off.

Where does the bot run?

Usually on a small cloud server close to the exchange, with monitoring and automatic restarts. MetaTrader bots can run on a Windows VPS.

Can you build a trading bot for Robinhood?

Robinhood offers an official Crypto Trading API and, since May 2026, an Agentic Trading beta in the US where AI agents connect through its MCP servers to a separate account. It has no general stock-trading API for custom code, so for a coded stock bot we usually suggest a broker with an official API, such as Alpaca, Interactive Brokers or Schwab.

Can a trading bot use Claude or ChatGPT?

Yes, for tasks such as summarising news into a signal, explaining the bot's activity or answering questions about your positions. Any trade a model suggests still passes the same hard risk limits, and every model input and output is logged. An AI step does not make a strategy profitable and must be tested like any other rule.

Do I get a dashboard for my trading bot?

If you want one. A typical trading bot dashboard shows positions, orders, daily profit and loss, risk limits and errors, with pause and kill-switch buttons. It usually adds 30 to 80 hours ($1,500 to $4,000) at $50 per hour.
