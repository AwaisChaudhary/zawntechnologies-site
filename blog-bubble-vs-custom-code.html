Bubble.io vs custom code: which is right for your marketplace?

By Awais Chaudhry, 19 November 2026

If you are launching a marketplace, one of the first decisions is whether to build it in a no-code platform like Bubble.io or write it in code. Both can work. The right answer depends on how fast you need to launch, how complex your marketplace is, and where you expect it to be in two years. We build in both, including a complete Bubble.io marketplace for a client in Australia, so this guide gives you the trade-offs as they look in real projects.

The short answer

  • Choose Bubble if you need to test the idea with real users in weeks, your marketplace follows a common pattern (listings, profiles, bookings, reviews, payments), and your budget is limited.
  • Choose custom code if your marketplace depends on complex search or matching, real-time features, heavy data, native mobile apps, strict compliance, or you already know it must scale quickly.
  • Consider a mix if most of the app is standard but one part is hard. Bubble can call custom services through APIs.

What a marketplace needs

Almost every two-sided marketplace needs the same core: sign-up for buyers and sellers, profiles, listings with photos, search and filters, booking or ordering, messaging, payments with payouts to sellers, reviews, notifications and an admin panel. Bubble covers most of this well out of the box or with plugins. Custom code covers all of it, but every piece has to be built or assembled.

Bubble.io: where it shines

Speed to launch

A focused marketplace MVP in Bubble often takes weeks rather than months, because user accounts, database, forms and admin views come built in. That speed matters most before you know whether the market wants your product.

Lower first cost

Fewer hours usually means a lower first invoice. Use our app cost calculator to see a custom-code range for your feature list, then expect a Bubble version of a standard marketplace to come in well below it.

Easy changes

Non-developers on your team can adjust text, layouts and simple workflows without waiting for a release, which helps when you are still learning what users want.

Bubble.io: where it struggles

  • Performance under load. Large searches, many filters and heavy pages can slow down, and Bubble's usage-based pricing can rise with traffic.
  • Complex logic. Matching algorithms, dynamic pricing or multi-step escrow rules get hard to manage in visual workflows.
  • Native mobile apps. Bubble now supports native mobile apps, but custom native or Flutter apps still give more control over offline use, performance and device features.
  • Ownership. Your app runs on Bubble's platform. You own your data, but you cannot take the app's code elsewhere.

Custom code: where it shines

  • No ceiling. Any feature, integration or algorithm is possible, and performance can be tuned for your traffic.
  • Full ownership. The code lives in your repository, runs on infrastructure you choose, and can be maintained by any competent team.
  • Predictable scaling costs. Hosting grows with usage, but you are not tied to one platform's pricing model.
  • Better fit for investors and acquirers who want to see a conventional, transferable codebase.

The trade-off is time and money up front. A custom marketplace usually takes several months and costs more for the first version. See our guide to app development costs in 2026 for realistic ranges.

How to decide: five questions

  1. Have you proven demand? If not, launch faster and cheaper, usually in Bubble.
  2. Is your core feature unusual? If the value of your marketplace is a clever matching, pricing or real-time feature, build that part in code.
  3. Do users mostly use a phone app? If yes and the app must work offline or use the camera heavily, lean toward custom mobile development.
  4. How much traffic do you expect in year one? Modest traffic suits Bubble; high traffic from day one suits custom code.
  5. Who will maintain it? A small non-technical team may prefer Bubble; a team with developers will move faster in code.

Building in Bubble so you can switch later

If you start in Bubble, a few habits make a later move much easier: keep the database clean and well named, avoid storing logic in page elements, use backend workflows and APIs for important processes, document how payments and payouts work, and keep payments in a service like Stripe Connect rather than custom workarounds. Then, when you outgrow Bubble, you can rebuild in stages instead of starting over.

Questions founders ask

Can a Bubble.io marketplace handle real traffic?

Yes, many marketplaces run on Bubble with thousands of users, as long as the database, privacy rules and workflows are structured well. Heavy real-time features, complex search and very high volumes are where Bubble starts to struggle and costs rise.

Is it cheaper to build a marketplace in Bubble?

Usually yes for the first version, often by half or more, because forms, user accounts and admin screens come built in. Over several years, platform fees and workarounds can narrow that gap, so compare total cost over two to three years, not just the build.

Can I move from Bubble to custom code later?

Yes, but it is a rebuild of the app, not an export. Clean data structure and documented workflows make the move faster. Many teams move in stages, starting with the parts Bubble handles worst, such as search or payments logic, through APIs.
