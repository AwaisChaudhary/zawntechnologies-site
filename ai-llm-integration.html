AI and LLM integration for products that already have users

We connect large language models to your data and workflows, so your product can answer questions, draft content and handle routine work without making things up.

Why teams hire us for this

Most AI projects stall at the demo stage. The model gives impressive answers in a notebook, then struggles with real customer data, cost limits and edge cases. Our work starts there: we design the retrieval, prompts, evaluation and fallbacks that make an AI feature dependable enough to ship.

We work with OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and open-source models, and we pick based on your accuracy, privacy and budget needs rather than habit.

What we build

  • Customer support and sales assistants trained on your own documents
  • Retrieval (RAG) search across PDFs, knowledge bases and databases
  • AI agents that call your APIs to book, update or look up records
  • Content generation tools with review and approval steps
  • Evaluation suites that measure answer quality before every release
  • Cost and latency tuning, including caching and smaller-model routing

How we work

Every engagement starts with a short discovery phase: we learn your users, your data and your constraints, then send a written plan with milestones and a fixed estimate per milestone. You see working software every one to two weeks, and the code lives in your repository from day one.

Our rate is $50 per hour. For fixed-scope work we quote per milestone so you know the cost before each stage starts.

Questions clients ask

How long does an AI feature take to build?

A focused feature such as a document assistant usually takes 3 to 6 weeks, including an evaluation set built from your real questions. Larger agent workflows take longer and we scope them in phases.

Will the AI make things up?

Any language model can. We reduce it by grounding answers in your documents, citing sources, refusing when the answer is not in the data, and testing against a fixed set of questions before each release.

Can we keep our data private?

Yes. We can use providers that do not train on your data, run models in your own cloud account, or deploy open-source models on your servers.
