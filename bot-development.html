What a bot is, and when it is worth building one

A bot is a program that watches for something and then acts on it without a person clicking buttons. It might watch a price and place an order, read a message and reply, or check a supplier's stock every hour and update your store. A custom bot is worth it when the task is repetitive, the rules can be written down, and a mistake or a delay costs you money or customers.

How a bot works

Almost every bot, whatever it does, follows the same five steps. Knowing them makes it easier to describe what you need and to understand a quote.

  1. 1. TriggerA schedule, a message, a webhook or a price change
  2. 2. DataRead from an API, a WebSocket stream, a database or a public page
  3. 3. Your rulesThe logic you define: if this, then that, within set limits
  4. 4. ActionPlace an order, send a reply, update a record or post an alert
  5. 5. Log and monitorEvery action is recorded; errors alert you; a kill switch stops it

The rules in step 3 are yours. Steps 1, 2, 4 and 5 are where most of the engineering time goes, because they have to work every time.

Features most bots need

  • A settings panel or simple commands (for example Telegram "/status" and "/pause") so you can control the bot without a developer
  • Limits: maximum order size, maximum messages per minute, daily caps
  • Automatic restart and recovery, so the bot remembers where it was after a crash
  • Logs you can read and alerts when something fails
  • Secure storage for API keys and passwords, with the narrowest permissions possible
  • A test mode (paper trading, a staging store or a test group) before it touches real money or customers

How we work

  1. Write down the rules. A short call and a written spec: what triggers the bot, what it does, what it must never do.
  2. Fixed estimate per milestone. You know the cost before each stage starts.
  3. Build and test safely. Paper trading, a test group or a staging store first. You see working software every one to two weeks.

  5. Support. Exchanges and platforms change their APIs; we can keep the bot updated on an hourly basis.

Risks, rules and what we do not build

A bot does exactly what it is told, quickly and repeatedly, so a bad rule repeats quickly too. That is why every bot we build has limits, a test mode and a kill switch.

  • Trading bots automate a strategy you define. They do not create profit on their own. Nobody can honestly promise returns, and trading carries a real risk of loss.
  • Platform terms and law. You are responsible for following the terms of the exchange, broker, marketplace or messaging platform the bot uses, and the laws where you and your users are. We build to official APIs wherever they exist.
  • What we turn down: fake engagement bots (automated likes, follows or comments), fake review or fake buyer bots, checkout scalper bots, and bots designed to get around a platform's limits or security.

Questions clients ask

How much does it cost to build a bot?

At $50 per hour, a simple WhatsApp or Telegram FAQ bot is usually 8 to 20 hours ($400 to $1,000), a trading alert bot 30 to 60 hours ($1,500 to $3,000), and a grid or DCA crypto bot with a control panel 60 to 120 hours ($3,000 to $6,000). Larger systems such as arbitrage cost more.

How long does it take to build a custom bot?

A simple bot is often ready in one to two weeks. A trading bot with backtesting and a dashboard usually takes four to eight weeks, including a paper-trading period before real money is used.

Can a trading bot guarantee profits?

No. A bot automates the strategy you define; it does not create an edge. We test on historical data and paper trade first, but trading always carries a risk of loss, and anyone who promises returns should be avoided.

Who owns the bot and the code?

You do. The code lives in your repository, the bot runs on your server or cloud account, and API keys stay with you. We sign an NDA on request.

Which programming languages do you use for bots?

Mostly Python and Node.js, with MQL4/MQL5 for MetaTrader and Pine Script for TradingView. We choose based on the platform's official libraries and what your team can maintain.

Do you build bots that break platform rules?

No. We do not build fake engagement, fake review, scalper or rule-evading bots. We build to official APIs and you follow the terms of each platform you use.
