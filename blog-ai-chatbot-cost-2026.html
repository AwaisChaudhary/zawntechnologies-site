How much does it cost to build an AI chatbot in 2026?

By Awais Chaudhry, 18 November 2026

An AI chatbot can cost a few thousand dollars or well over fifty thousand. The difference is not the chat window. It is what the bot has to know, what it is allowed to do, and how sure you need to be that its answers are right. This guide breaks the cost down the way we estimate chatbot projects at ZAWN Technologies, using our own rate of $50 per hour.

The three kinds of AI chatbot, and what each costs

Almost every chatbot request we receive falls into one of three groups. These are planning ranges for a first version, not fixed quotes.

Type of chatbotTypical hoursAt $50 per hour
Answer bot: answers questions from a set of web pages or FAQs, collects leads, hands over to a human80 to 160$4,000 to $8,000
Document-trained assistant: searches your PDFs, help center or database and cites its sources, with an admin panel to update content200 to 400$10,000 to $20,000
Action-taking agent: books, refunds, updates CRM records or places orders through your systems, with approval steps and logs400 to 900$20,000 to $45,000

Voice bots, talking avatars and bots that must work in several languages add to these ranges. So does any requirement to run the model on your own servers instead of a hosted provider.

Where the hours actually go

Connecting a chat window to a language model takes a day. The rest of the budget goes into the parts that make the bot safe to put in front of customers.

Preparing your content

The bot can only be as good as what it reads. Old help articles, duplicate PDFs and contradictory policies produce contradictory answers. Cleaning and structuring content is often 15 to 25 percent of a document-trained project.

Retrieval: finding the right passage

A document-trained assistant does not memorise your files. It searches them for each question and gives the model the most relevant passages, a method called retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). Tuning that search, so the bot finds the right paragraph instead of a similar-sounding one, is where answer quality is won or lost.

Testing against real questions

We build a set of real customer questions with the correct answers and run the bot against it before every release. Without this, nobody can say whether a change made the bot better or worse. Expect 10 to 20 percent of the budget here, and more for regulated industries.

Guardrails and handover

The bot needs to refuse topics it should not discuss, say when it does not know, and pass the conversation to a person with the full history. Action-taking agents also need limits, approval steps for anything irreversible and a log of every action.

Integrations

Each system the bot talks to, such as a CRM, booking calendar, order database or WhatsApp, adds setup, error handling and testing. Integrations are the most common reason a project grows beyond its first estimate.

Running costs after launch

Unlike most software, a chatbot has a cost every time someone uses it. Plan for four items:

  • Model usage. Providers such as OpenAI, Anthropic and Google charge by the amount of text processed. Long documents passed in with every question cost more than short, well-chosen passages, which is one more reason good retrieval matters.
  • Hosting and database. A small server or serverless functions, plus a database for documents and conversation history.
  • Channel fees. WhatsApp Business and some help desk platforms charge per conversation or per seat.
  • Maintenance. Updating content, reviewing failed conversations and moving to newer models. Budget a few hours a month for a small bot.

Before building, we estimate monthly running cost from your expected number of conversations, and we keep it down with caching, shorter prompts and smaller models for simple questions.

How to lower the cost without lowering quality

  • Start with one job. A bot that answers order-status and returns questions well is worth more than one that half-answers everything.
  • Use the content you have. Fixing your top 30 help articles often does more for answer quality than any technical change.
  • Keep a human in the loop at first. Let the bot draft answers that an agent approves for the first few weeks, then automate the categories it gets right.
  • Do not build what you can rent. Use managed services for chat widgets, authentication and hosting where they fit, and spend the budget on accuracy.

Builder platform or custom chatbot?

No-code chatbot builders are a good choice for a simple FAQ bot on a small website. Custom development makes sense when the bot must read your private documents reliably, take actions in your own systems, meet data privacy requirements, or when per-conversation platform fees grow larger than the cost of owning the software. Questions clients ask

How much does a simple AI chatbot cost?

A website chatbot that answers from a fixed set of pages and hands over to a human usually takes 80 to 160 hours. At our rate of $50 per hour that is about $4,000 to $8,000, plus monthly model and hosting costs.

What does it cost to run an AI chatbot each month?

Running cost is mostly model usage, charged per amount of text processed, plus hosting and a vector database. A small business bot often costs tens of dollars a month; a busy support bot can cost hundreds or more. We estimate it from your expected number of conversations before building.

Is it cheaper to use a chatbot builder platform?

For a basic FAQ bot, often yes. Custom development pays off when the bot must read your own documents reliably, take actions in your systems, meet privacy rules, or when platform fees per conversation become larger than the cost of owning the software.
