What makes a bot cost more?

  • Unclear rules. "Buy when the trend turns" has to become exact conditions, sizes and exits before it can be coded. Working this out together is useful, but it takes hours.
  • More platforms. Each extra exchange, broker, marketplace or chat platform adds its own API, rate limits and failure cases to handle and test.
  • Real money and real customers. Trading and order bots need limits, a kill switch, recovery after a crash and checks that orders really filled. This is most of the work, and we do not skip it.
  • Other users. Letting other people sign up and connect their own accounts adds logins, billing, support and, for trading, legal questions that need advice in your country.

What does a bot cost to run each month?

Most simple bots run on a small cloud server for about $5 to $40 a month. Trading and arbitrage bots usually need a steadier VPS close to the exchange or broker, often $20 to $80 a month, and a web dashboard adds a little more. Maintenance covers API changes, library updates and fixes; exchanges and platforms change their APIs often, so we suggest budgeting a few hours a month. Exchange or broker fees, data feeds, AI model usage and WhatsApp Business fees are extra and set by those providers.

What this calculator does not tell you

It estimates what a bot costs to build and run, not what it will earn. A bot follows the strategy you define; it does not make a strategy profitable, and trading carries real risk of loss. You stay responsible for following your exchange, broker or platform terms and the laws where you live. We do not build betting bots, fake engagement or review bots, checkout scalper bots or meme-coin sniper bots.

Questions about bot development cost

How much does it cost to build a bot?

At $50 per hour, a simple Telegram or WhatsApp FAQ bot is about $400 to $1,000, a Discord moderation bot $750 to $2,000, a trading signal bot or MT5 Expert Advisor $1,500 to $3,000, a grid or DCA crypto bot $3,000 to $6,000, and a cross-exchange arbitrage bot $7,500 to $15,000 or more. Dashboards, backtesting, extra platforms and user accounts add to these ranges.

How much does a crypto trading bot cost?

A webhook or signal bot on one exchange is usually 30 to 60 hours ($1,500 to $3,000). A grid or DCA bot with a settings panel and alerts is 60 to 120 hours ($3,000 to $6,000). Backtesting, a web dashboard and each extra exchange add hours. Running cost is mostly a VPS at about $20 to $80 a month plus exchange fees.

How much does a NinjaTrader strategy or TradingView bridge cost?

At $50 per hour, a NinjaTrader 8 strategy in NinjaScript from clear rules on one futures instrument is usually 30 to 60 hours ($1,500 to $3,000), the same range as an MT5 Expert Advisor. A bridge that takes TradingView alerts and places orders in NinjaTrader or MT5 needs a small server receiver plus code on the platform side, usually 40 to 80 hours ($2,000 to $4,000). Prop-firm loss guards and Telegram alerts add to both.

How much does a web scraper or scraper bot cost?

At $50 per hour, a scraper for one simple public site with a CSV or Google Sheets export is usually 8 to 20 hours ($400 to $1,000). A scheduled scraper bot for a JavaScript-heavy site with change alerts is usually 20 to 50 hours ($1,000 to $2,500), and a multi-site pipeline with a database, deduplication and a dashboard 60 to 150 hours ($3,000 to $7,500). Servers, proxies and data services are paid separately, often $10 to $50 a month for small jobs. We use official APIs where they exist and do not bypass logins, paywalls or CAPTCHAs.

How accurate is this bot cost calculator?

It gives a planning range from the hours ZAWN Technologies uses in first estimates for bot projects. Your quote depends on the exact rules, the platforms and how polished the control panel needs to be, so treat the result as a starting point and ask us for a milestone-by-milestone estimate.

Can the calculator tell me how much my trading bot will make?

No. It only estimates build and running costs. No honest developer can promise returns: a bot carries out the strategy you define, and trading carries risk of loss. We recommend backtesting and paper trading before using real money, then starting small.

Why is the timeline so short for small bots?

The timeline assumes one developer working about 30 hours a week on your bot, with at least one week for testing and your feedback. Bots that touch real money also get a paper-trading or test period before going live, which can add calendar time.

Do you charge a monthly fee?

No fixed fee is required. The code and the server are yours. If you want us to keep the bot updated when APIs change, we agree a small number of maintenance hours a month at $50 per hour.

How much does a trade copier or Telegram signal copier cost?

At $50 per hour, an MT4 or MT5 copier from one master to several of your accounts on one VPS is usually 25 to 50 hours ($1,250 to $2,500). A Telegram signal copier that turns one channel's message format into MT5 or exchange orders is about 40 to 80 hours ($2,000 to $4,000). Copying across brokers, platforms or machines through a copy server is about 60 to 120 hours ($3,000 to $6,000). See trade copier development.
