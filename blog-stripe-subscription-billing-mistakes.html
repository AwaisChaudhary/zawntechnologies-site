Stripe subscriptions: common billing mistakes and how to avoid them

By Awais Chaudhry, 23 November 2026

Quick answer: most Stripe subscription problems come from the same handful of mistakes: granting access on the checkout redirect instead of on webhooks, not verifying or de-duplicating webhook events, ignoring failed payments and 3D Secure, messy proration on plan changes, and keeping your own copy of subscription state that drifts away from Stripe. A solid integration with Stripe Checkout, the Customer Portal and properly handled webhooks is usually 30 to 60 hours of work, about $1,500 to $3,000 at $50 per hour; custom pricing with seats, usage billing and tax is more.

Stripe Billing does a lot for you: it creates invoices, charges cards on schedule, retries failed payments and emails receipts. That makes it easy to think the integration is finished once the first test payment goes through. In practice the bugs appear weeks later, when a card expires, a customer upgrades mid-month, or a webhook arrives twice. This guide lists the mistakes we see most often when we build or fix subscription billing, and what to do instead. Check Stripe's own documentation and pricing page for current details, because the platform changes often.

How do Stripe subscriptions actually work?

Four objects matter. A Product is what you sell (for example "Pro plan"). A Price is how you charge for it: amount, currency and interval, such as $29 per month or $290 per year. A Customer holds the person's details and saved payment methods. A Subscription links a customer to one or more prices and creates an Invoice each billing period, which Stripe then tries to pay.

Your app does not charge anyone directly. It creates the subscription (usually through Stripe Checkout), then listens to webhooks, which are messages Stripe sends to your server when something happens: a payment succeeded, a payment failed, the plan changed, the subscription ended. Your app grants or removes access based on those messages. Almost every mistake below is a variation of getting that loop wrong.

Common Stripe subscription mistakes and how to avoid them

1. Granting access on the success page instead of the webhook

After checkout, Stripe redirects the customer to your success URL. It is tempting to unlock the account there. But the customer can close the tab before the redirect, the page can be reloaded or shared, and some payment methods confirm later. Treat the success page as a "thanks, we are setting things up" screen. Grant access when your server receives checkout.session.completed and, for renewals, invoice.paid.

2. Not verifying webhook signatures

Your webhook endpoint is a public URL. If it does not check the Stripe-Signature header with your endpoint's signing secret, anyone who finds it can post a fake "payment succeeded" event. Every official Stripe library has a helper to verify the signature; use it, and keep the signing secret out of your code repository.

3. Assuming each webhook arrives once and in order

Stripe may send the same event more than once, and events do not always arrive in the order they happened. If your handler adds a month of credit every time it sees invoice.paid, a duplicate gives a free month. Store the event ID and skip events you have already processed. When order matters, do not trust the event payload alone: fetch the current subscription from the Stripe API and act on that.

4. Doing slow work inside the webhook handler

Stripe expects a quick 2xx response. If your handler sends emails, generates PDFs and calls three other services before replying, it will time out, Stripe will retry, and you get duplicates (see mistake 3). Save the event, return 200 straight away, and process it in a background job.

5. Ignoring failed payments

Cards expire, get replaced or hit limits. When a renewal fails the subscription usually moves to past_due, and Stripe can retry the payment on a schedule you choose (Smart Retries). The mistake is either cutting access instantly on the first failure or never cutting it at all. Decide a policy, for example a grace period while retries run, with emails asking the customer to update their card, then restrict access if the subscription becomes unpaid or canceled. Listen to invoice.payment_failed so your app can show a clear "update your payment method" banner.

6. Forgetting 3D Secure and incomplete subscriptions

In the UK, the EU and some other regions, banks often require customers to confirm payments with 3D Secure under Strong Customer Authentication rules. A first payment that needs confirmation leaves the subscription incomplete; if it is not confirmed in time it becomes incomplete_expired. Renewals can also need authentication. Stripe Checkout and the hosted invoice page handle the confirmation screens, which is one strong reason to use them. If you build a custom payment form, you must handle these states yourself.

7. Getting upgrades, downgrades and proration wrong

When a customer moves from a $29 to a $99 plan halfway through the month, Stripe by default calculates a prorated adjustment. Questions you need to answer before writing code:

  • Should upgrades charge the difference immediately, or on the next invoice?
  • Should downgrades take effect now (with a credit) or at the end of the paid period? Most SaaS products downgrade at period end.
  • What happens to features and data the customer loses on a downgrade?
  • Can customers switch between monthly and yearly, and how is the unused time handled?

Show the customer the amount before they confirm. Stripe can preview the next invoice for a proposed change, so the number on screen matches the charge.

8. Keeping your own subscription state that drifts from Stripe

You do need a local record of each customer's plan and status so your app can check access quickly. The mistake is letting that record change from anywhere other than Stripe events. If an admin edits the plan in your database but not in Stripe, or a refund in the Stripe Dashboard is never reflected in your app, the two disagree and someone gets billed for the wrong thing. Make Stripe the source of truth, update your copy only from webhooks or API reads, and run a periodic reconciliation job that compares the two.

9. Hard-coding price IDs and amounts

Prices in Stripe cannot have their amount edited; to change a price you create a new Price and move customers to it. If price IDs are scattered through your code and the amounts are typed into your pricing page, every price change becomes a deployment and a source of mismatch. Keep price IDs in configuration, map them to plan names in one place, and load displayed prices from that same source. Decide up front whether existing subscribers keep their old price ("grandfathering") when you raise prices.

10. Not testing renewals, trials and time

A single test payment proves very little. The bugs live in what happens next month. Stripe's test clocks let you create a test customer and move time forward, so you can watch a trial end, a renewal succeed, a renewal fail and retries run, all in minutes. Test with Stripe's special test cards for declines and 3D Secure, and test the webhook endpoint locally with the Stripe CLI.

11. Building a billing portal you did not need

Many teams spend days on screens for updating cards, downloading invoices, switching plans and cancelling. Stripe's hosted Customer Portal does all of this, can be configured for which plans customers may switch between, and supports cancellation at period end. Start with it; build custom screens only when the portal genuinely cannot do what you need.

12. Treating tax, refunds and cancellations as an afterthought

  • Tax: selling digital subscriptions across borders can mean collecting VAT, GST or sales tax. Stripe Tax can calculate and collect it, but you still need to collect customer addresses and get advice from an accountant on where you must register.
  • Refunds: decide whether a refund also cancels the subscription and removes access, and make sure your app reacts to it.
  • Cancellations: "cancel at period end" keeps access until the paid period is over; "cancel now" ends it immediately. Your app should handle both, and listen to customer.subscription.deleted to remove access when the subscription actually ends.

Which Stripe webhook events do you need for subscriptions?

EventWhat your app should do
checkout.session.completedLink the Stripe customer and subscription to your user; grant access once payment is confirmed.
invoice.paidExtend or confirm access for the new period; record the payment.
invoice.payment_failedNotify the customer, show an "update payment method" message, start your grace period.
customer.subscription.updatedSync plan, quantity, status and period end after upgrades, downgrades or status changes.
customer.subscription.deletedRemove paid access when the subscription has ended.
customer.subscription.trial_will_endRemind the customer before a free trial converts to a paid plan.

Stripe in mobile apps: the app store rule

If your iOS or Android app sells digital content or features used inside the app, Apple's and Google's store rules generally require their own in-app purchase systems, and their rules and regional exceptions change. Physical goods and real-world services can usually use Stripe. Many subscription businesses sell on the web with Stripe and let the mobile app sign in to an existing account, but check the current store guidelines for your case before you build. Our mobile app development team plans this at the scoping stage, because it affects pricing and architecture.

A short pre-launch checklist

  1. Webhook signatures verified, events de-duplicated, handler returns 200 quickly.
  2. Access granted and removed only from webhook events or API reads.
  3. Trial end, renewal, failed renewal, retries and cancellation tested with test clocks.
  4. Decline and 3D Secure test cards tried on the real checkout flow.
  5. Upgrade and downgrade rules written down and shown to customers before they confirm.
  6. Customer Portal configured, or custom screens tested.
  7. Live mode keys, webhook endpoint and signing secret set separately from test mode.
  8. A reconciliation job or report comparing your records with Stripe.

Questions about Stripe subscriptions

Should I use Stripe Checkout or build my own payment form?

For most subscription products, use Stripe Checkout and the Customer Portal. They handle card entry, 3D Secure, many payment methods, plan switching and invoice downloads, and Stripe keeps them up to date. Build a custom form only when you need a checkout experience Checkout cannot provide.

What happens when a customer's subscription payment fails?

The invoice stays open and the subscription usually becomes past_due. Stripe can retry the payment on a schedule and email the customer. Your app should listen to invoice.payment_failed, ask the customer to update their card, and restrict access only after your grace period or when the subscription becomes unpaid or canceled.

How do I test Stripe subscription renewals without waiting a month?

Use Stripe test clocks. Create a test customer and subscription attached to a test clock, then advance the clock to trigger trial endings, renewals, failed payments and retries in minutes, while your webhook endpoint receives the same events it would in live mode.
